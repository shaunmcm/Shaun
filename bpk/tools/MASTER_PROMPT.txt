4. THE MOST IMPORTANT RULE: HOOKS MUST PROMISE A SPECIFIC PAYOFF
Every Reel and carousel MUST begin with an original, emotionally engaging, curiosity-driven hook.
But curiosity alone is not enough.
The hook must clearly identify the specific problem the content will solve.
The viewer should understand three things immediately:
1.	What money-related parenting problem are we discussing?
2.	Why should this problem matter to them?
3.	What useful answer, transformation or explanation will they receive if they keep watching or swiping?
Never create a mystery that the content does not resolve.
Never end a Reel without answering the original hook.
Do not make viewers read the caption to obtain the solution promised in the video.
ROTATE THESE EIGHT HOOK MECHANISMS
MECHANISM 1 — CURIOSITY
Open with an unanswered question about a real parenting problem.
Example:
"Why does your child spend their allowance the moment they get it?"
Payoff: Explain one likely contributing factor and demonstrate a practical exercise to help children plan spending.
MECHANISM 2 — SURPRISING CONTRAST
Contrast a familiar parenting strategy with a more useful alternative.
Example:
"Teaching your child to save every dollar sounds responsible. But what are they learning about spending?"
Payoff: Explain the value of teaching both saving and thoughtful discretionary spending.
MECHANISM 3 — SPECIFIC PROBLEM
Identify an extremely relatable frustration.
Example:
"Your child asks for Robux every week. Here's how to teach them what those purchases actually cost."
Payoff: Demonstrate how to translate game currency into real money and compare purchases.
MECHANISM 4 — THE MISTAKE
Identify a common mistake without shaming parents.
Example:
"If you replace your child's allowance every time they spend it, they may miss this money lesson."
Payoff: Explain how predictable allowances and appropriate limits allow children to practice making trade-offs.
MECHANISM 5 — TRANSFORMATION
Show how a child or family could improve a financial behavior.
Example:
"From spending every dollar to saving for something special: try this simple allowance routine."
Payoff: Give parents a practical, age-appropriate routine.
MECHANISM 6 — STRONG OPINION
Challenge conventional parenting advice with a defensible, nuanced position.
Example:
"Kids don't need to be perfect savers. They need opportunities to make small money decisions."
Payoff: Explain why low-stakes practice helps children understand consequences.
MECHANISM 7 — NARRATIVE TENSION
Start a short hypothetical scenario involving a financial decision.
Example:
"A child has $20, but wants two toys that cost $15 each. What should the parent do?"
Payoff: Show how to guide the child toward comparing options instead of simply choosing for them.
MECHANISM 8 — USEFUL PROMISE
Clearly promise one immediately useful teaching method.
Example:
"Here's a two-minute game that can help your child understand needs versus wants."
Payoff: Demonstrate the game completely.
STRICT HOOK QUALITY RULES
Do not use:
•	"You won't believe what happened!"
•	"This changes everything!"
•	"Parents are shocked!"
•	"Nobody talks about this!"
•	"The secret they don't want you to know!"
•	"Do this before it's too late!"
Unless specific factual evidence supports the claim, avoid these types of empty sensational statements.
No misleading fear, invented statistics or guaranteed child development outcomes.
The goal is earned curiosity, not clickbait.
Before creating each post, generate five substantially different hook candidates using different mechanisms.
Score each candidate from 1–10 for:
•	Immediate clarity.
•	Curiosity.
•	Emotional relevance.
•	Specificity of the problem.
•	Strength of the promised payoff.
•	Visual storytelling potential.
•	Accuracy and credibility.
•	Relevance to the book and audience.
Choose the strongest hook that is accurate, original and executable.
Rotate mechanisms throughout the week to avoid repetitiveness.

REQUIRED REEL STRUCTURE

Immediately display the hook in large, highly legible on-screen text.
Identify the actual parenting problem.
Do not start with an intro logo, greeting, background music alone or a generic quote.

SCENE 2 — 0:02–0:06: CREATE TENSION
Explain why the problem occurs or introduce a misconception.

SCENE 3 — 0:06–0:13: INTRODUCE THE SOLUTION
Begin teaching the actual money lesson.
Example text:

Visual: Two envelopes and a child's allowance.

SCENE 4 — 0:13–0:21: DEMONSTRATE THE SOLUTION

Visual: Money moving between two labeled envelopes.
The demonstration must be easy to understand without needing to open the caption.
SCENE 5 — 0:21–0:28: DELIVER THE PAYOFF
Resolve the original problem clearly.
Example text:
"The lesson isn't just saving money. It's learning what every spending choice costs."
Visual: A child-sized savings envelope gradually filling toward a toy purchase.
The viewer should receive a useful insight or actionable solution.
SCENE 6 — 0:28–0:34: CALL TO ACTION
Connect the lesson naturally to BrokeProofKids.
Example:
"Want more money activities you can do together? Explore our kids' money activity book. Link in bio."
Visual: A verified image of the actual book when authorized assets are available, or a clean branded CTA screen.
